Okay. Seriously.

scAIm™ doesn't exist. But you've surely run into its cousin this week on your LinkedIn or X feed: an “AI architect”, an “agent wizard”, a pretty animation, a counter going up, and zero detail about what is actually done, how, with which data, and what happens when it gets it wrong.

I'm a developer. I use AI every day, and it is genuinely useful when it answers a real, well-posed problem. That is exactly why I don't like seeing it sold as a magic trick.

When someone shows me this kind of tool, I always ask the same questions. Where will my data go, and who will be able to read it? What happens if it gets it wrong while processing it: an email sent to the wrong customer, a digit flipped in a quote, a forgotten file… and who will notice? Have I seen it run on a real case, or only on the demo? When nobody can answer me simply, I conclude there isn't much behind it.

Honestly, a good share of the “AI needs” people describe to me can be solved with a small script, a well-made export or a clean spreadsheet. It's less exciting on LinkedIn, but it works, it costs next to nothing, and you know what's inside.

A good tech project, AI or not, starts with a conversation: understanding your business, your constraints, what makes you lose time. Then code that works, that can be read, tested and maintained. And sometimes the right answer is: “you don't need AI for that”.

No sorcery. Craftsmanship.
